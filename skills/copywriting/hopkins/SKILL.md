# Hopkins: scientific advertising and measurable selling

Derived from Emmanuel's internal paraphrased framework based on Claude C. Hopkins's *Scientific Advertising*.

## Core thesis

Marketing ideas are hypotheses until buyers respond.

Do not ask only whether copy is creative. Ask whether it attracts the right prospect, creates the intended action, and produces acceptable business results.

## Salesmanship rule

Treat the ad, landing page, email, or sales sequence like a salesperson operating at scale.

Use plain, sincere, useful language.

**Salesman test:** Would this statement help if the buyer were standing in front of us?

If not, remove or rewrite it.

## Specificity

Replace vague claims with verified facts where possible.

Do not manufacture numbers merely to sound specific.

Examples:
- "faster setup" -> measured setup time if actually tested;
- "lower cost" -> exact price or verified comparison;
- "more reliable" -> documented process, guarantee, test, or measured result;
- "complete" -> named shipped capabilities.

## Research before copy

Use available evidence such as:
- customer interviews;
- sales/support conversations;
- reviews and objections;
- search behavior;
- competitor offers;
- product usage;
- pricing;
- purchase and retention behavior.

The creator's view is not automatically the buyer's view.

## Testing doctrine

When a meaningful question can be answered by a controlled test, prefer testing over internal argument.

Potential variables:
- headline;
- offer;
- CTA;
- price presentation;
- trial;
- guarantee/risk reversal;
- proof placement;
- visual argument;
- message angle;
- audience segment;
- sequence.

Record the context and result.

Do not declare a winner from noisy early data.

## Measurement hierarchy

Measure as close to business value as the product allows:

1. Qualified attention
2. CTA response
3. Lead or trial
4. Activation
5. Checkout start
6. Purchase
7. Cost per customer
8. Revenue per customer
9. Retention/repeat purchase
10. Refund/churn/failure
11. Payback period
12. Contribution margin/profit where available

Never optimize an upstream metric while damaging downstream customer quality.

## Offer and risk reversal

Trials, demos, samples, previews, guarantees, guided onboarding, or starter packages should reduce uncertainty, not exist because "free converts."

Test their effect on conversion, perceived value, activation, support load, and customer quality.

## Visual-sales rule

Every visual should justify its attention, space, or performance cost by:
- demonstrating the product;
- clarifying the mechanism;
- visualizing an outcome;
- reducing uncertainty;
- supporting a claim.

Decoration alone is not a sales argument.

## Required workflow

1. Product truth audit
2. Buyer research
3. Define the sales job
4. Define primary and downstream metrics
5. Build the argument
6. Remove waste
7. Create a testable hypothesis
8. Run the smallest useful test
9. Judge downstream economics
10. Record learning
11. Scale or iterate

## Before finalizing

State:
- sales job;
- intended buyer;
- strongest specific claim;
- proof available;
- primary success metric;
- downstream quality metric;
- test hypothesis.

Unknowns stay unknown.
