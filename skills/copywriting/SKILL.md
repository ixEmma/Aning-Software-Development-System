# Aning copywriting router

Use for product pages, landing pages, SaaS, books, ads, email, and other persuasive communication.

Do not automatically use every framework.

## First diagnose

Before drafting, establish:
- product truth;
- intended buyer;
- medium;
- primary sales job;
- buyer awareness;
- market sophistication;
- available proof;
- measurement needs.

Never invent missing product facts, proof, testimonials, results, scarcity, urgency, partnerships, or numbers.

## Route by problem

### Schwartz: what to say

Use `schwartz/SKILL.md` when the problem is market strategy:
- positioning;
- dominant desire;
- awareness;
- sophistication;
- headline angle;
- mechanism;
- belief path;
- deciding what argument the market is ready to hear.

Primary question: **What existing desire are we channeling, and what argument will this market believe now?**

### Sugarman: how to say it

Use `sugarman/SKILL.md` when the argument is reasonably clear but the copy needs:
- sequencing;
- momentum;
- curiosity;
- readability;
- emotional relevance;
- objection handling;
- CTA progression.

Primary question: **How does each line or section earn the next one?**

### Hopkins: prove and measure it

Use `hopkins/SKILL.md` when the problem is:
- specificity;
- proof;
- measurable conversion;
- A/B testing;
- offer/risk reversal;
- acquisition economics;
- downstream customer quality.

Primary question: **What sales job must this asset perform, and what evidence will tell us whether it worked?**

## Combined order

For major conversion work where all three materially matter:

1. Product truth and customer research
2. Schwartz: desire, awareness, sophistication, dominant performance, mechanism
3. Sugarman: concept, sequence, momentum, objections, CTA flow
4. Hopkins: specificity, proof, test design, business metrics
5. Controlled launch
6. Measure
7. Iterate

Do not use all three automatically.

## When not to use

Do not invoke persuasion frameworks automatically for neutral documentation, academic writing, factual reports, legal text, support instructions, or ordinary technical references.

For SEO content, satisfy search intent and factual usefulness first. Use persuasion only where the page has a legitimate conversion job.

## Final-copy punctuation rule

In persuasive or marketing prose:
- do not use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation;
- do not use standalone hyphens as punctuation;
- use commas, periods, colons, semicolons, or parentheses instead;
- keep hyphens only inside genuine compound words or literal technical strings.
