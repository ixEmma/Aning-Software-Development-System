# Sugarman: slippery slide and buyer psychology

Derived from Emmanuel's internal paraphrased framework based on Joseph Sugarman's *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*.

## Core principles

1. Know the product before writing.
2. Know the prospect.
3. Sell the larger concept, not a feature dump.
4. Make every element earn the next action.
5. Create a buying environment appropriate to the audience and offer.
6. Build agreement with truthful, recognizable realities.
7. Reduce friction in the reading sequence.
8. Use curiosity to aid momentum, not hide information.
9. Let emotion create motivation and logic justify the decision.
10. Answer objections in the order they naturally arise.
11. Use as much copy as the decision requires, no more.
12. Write like one person speaking clearly to another.
13. Edit for precision.
14. Match the medium.
15. Test rather than worship rules.

## Workflow

### 1. Product truth audit
Verify shipped capabilities, limitations, price, proof, integrations, and desired action.

### 2. Prospect map
Capture current situation, desired outcome, frustrations, fears, alternatives, objections, sophistication, and vocabulary.

### 3. Concept statement
Complete:

```text
The customer is not really buying [feature/product].
They are buying [larger outcome, relief, identity, advantage, or experience].
```

### 4. Persuasion sequence
A useful default for modern landing pages:

```text
attention
-> recognition
-> problem/desire
-> possibility
-> product concept
-> mechanism/features
-> proof
-> objections
-> offer
-> action
```

Change the sequence when the audience or medium requires it.

### 5. Momentum draft
Write the full argument before over-editing. Each section should answer the current question and create a legitimate reason to continue.

### 6. Proof and justification
Support desire with verified detail, demonstrations, comparisons, testimonials, guarantees, pricing logic, or other real evidence.

### 7. Objection pass
After every major claim ask: "What would a serious buyer question next?"

Answer with the minimum truthful information required.

### 8. Tightening pass
Remove repetition, vague jargon, weak transitions, and anything that does not improve meaning, persuasion, proof, rhythm, or momentum.

## SaaS translation

Infrastructure should normally support a larger concept such as:
- starting further ahead;
- reducing repetitive setup;
- lowering uncertainty;
- increasing ownership/control.

Do not lead with a wall of features unless the audience truly needs it.

## Audit

- Does the opening earn the next line?
- Does the buyer recognize themselves?
- Are we selling a concept rather than dumping features?
- Is every material claim supported?
- Are objections answered at the right time?
- Does curiosity improve understanding?
- Is any section present only because "landing pages have one"?
- Is the CTA the natural next step?
