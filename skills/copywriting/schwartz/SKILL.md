# Schwartz: market desire, awareness, and sophistication

Derived from Emmanuel's internal paraphrased framework based on Eugene M. Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*.

## Core thesis

Copy does not manufacture desire from nothing. Identify a strong existing desire and connect the product's legitimate performance to it.

Do not start with "What headline sounds good?"

Start with:
1. What existing desire creates this market?
2. How aware is the prospect?
3. How sophisticated is the market?

## Product analysis

Separate:
- **physical product**: what is delivered;
- **functional product**: what it does for the buyer.

Lead with the strongest functional performance tied to the strongest relevant desire. Use features and specifications to explain and prove that performance.

## Dominant performance

Do not give every feature equal weight.

Choose one dominant performance that best combines:
- urgency;
- staying power;
- market scope;
- product truth.

Supporting benefits should strengthen it, not compete with it.

## Awareness routing

### Most aware
Lead directly with product, offer, price, availability, or reason to act.

### Product aware
Strengthen desire, superiority, proof, mechanism, or removed limitation.

### Solution aware
Lead with the desired result or solution, then connect it to the product mechanism.

### Problem aware
Lead from the recognized problem or desire into the solution category, then the product.

### Unaware
Begin with identity, situation, emotion, aspiration, tension, or recognizable reality. Bridge gradually toward problem, solution, and product.

Do not use a headline style designed for one awareness level on another.

## Sophistication routing

- **Early market**: a direct credible promise may be enough.
- **Growing competition**: sharpen the promise only if product truth supports it.
- **Mechanism market**: explain why this product can produce the result differently.
- **Highly sophisticated market**: use a more specific mechanism, proof structure, limitation-removal story, or positioning.
- **Resistant market**: use identification, experience, worldview, or fresh framing rather than shouting the same exhausted claim.

## Belief architecture

Build conviction gradually:

```text
accepted reality -> implication -> explanation -> mechanism -> proof -> stronger conclusion
```

Place proof where skepticism appears instead of dumping it into one generic section.

## Useful techniques

- **Intensification**: make a real benefit more vivid or specific.
- **Identification**: connect performance to identity or status where legitimate.
- **Gradualization**: build belief in acceptable steps.
- **Redefinition**: reframe a real characteristic without hiding disadvantages.
- **Mechanization**: explain why the product should work.
- **Concentration**: show where alternatives fail and how the product removes those weaknesses, factually.
- **Camouflage**: use a format/tone the audience naturally accepts without disguising advertising as something else.

## Output before drafting

State:
- mass desire;
- awareness level;
- sophistication level;
- dominant performance;
- mechanism;
- strongest available proof;
- opening strategy.

Then write.
